Data sources

Everything is placed in one common space, MNI152NLin2009cAsym (millimetres, RAS). The fsaverage surfaces are moved from MNI305 with FreeSurfer's published affine.

  • Cortical surfaces: FreeSurfer fsaverage, 163,842 vertices per hemisphere (pial, white, sphere, sulc, curv). Retrieved via TemplateFlow. The inflated surfaces are computed here by area-preserving Laplacian smoothing of the white surface.
  • Cortical atlases: Desikan–Killiany (Desikan 2006); Destrieux (2010); Yeo 7/17 networks (2011); Schaefer 100/400/1000 (2018); Brainnetome (Fan 2016); Lausanne 2018 (Cammoun 2012). All from TemplateFlow. Glasser HCP-MMP1 (Glasser 2016), as projected onto fsaverage by Kathryn Mills (figshare, CC BY 4.0).
  • Volumes: ICBM 2009c nonlinear asymmetric template (Fonov 2009/2011): T1, T2, tissue probabilities, and an aseg-style segmentation. Also the MASSP 2.0 subcortical atlas (Bazin 2025) and the MIAL thalamic nuclei (Najdenovska 2018). All from TemplateFlow.
  • Body map: the motor (area 4) and touch (areas 1/2/3) subregions of the Brainnetome atlas, grouped by body part. The zones between body parts are described by Gordon et al. 2023.
  • TMS targets: published targets placed on the template. Depression: Fox et al. 2012 (the 5-cm rule, F3 after Herwig 2003, and the connectivity target), Beam 2009, Cole 2020/2022 (SAINT). Motor hotspot: Mayka 2006. OCD: Carmi 2018/2019. Smoking: Zangen 2021. Migraine: Lipton 2010. The right-DLPFC point mirrors F3, and the deep-coil targets are atlas-region centres. These are group averages for learning; clinics locate each patient's target individually.
  • Tracts: the HCP-842 population-averaged tractography atlas (Yeh 2018), as curated into 80 bundles by Eleftherios Garyfallidis (CC BY 4.0). Streamlines are subsampled and simplified for the browser.
  • Signal flow: an illustrative model, not a recording. Diffusion MRI can't tell which way a fibre conducts, so each bundle's direction comes from textbook neuroanatomy: projection, cerebellar, brainstem and cranial-nerve pathways one way, association and commissural bundles both ways. Each streamline end is assigned the Desikan–Killiany area or deep nucleus it reaches. Timing is modelled: one conduction speed (3.9 m/s, the median that the F-TRACT stimulation atlas measured between cortical areas, Lemaréchal 2022), 1.5 ms per relay, shown slowed down. The deep-sleep wave follows Massimini et al. 2004.
  • Stimulation responses: measured. The F-TRACT atlas v2112 (INSERM; Zenodo), from cortico-cortical evoked potentials recorded with intracranial electrodes in 780 epilepsy patients: response probability, onset latency and axonal conduction delay between the Lausanne2008-33 areas, ages 15 and up. Free for research use only. Cite David et al. 2013 and Trebaul et al. 2018 (NeuroImage), and Lemaréchal et al. 2022 (Brain). Pairs tested fewer than the chosen number of times are left out; pulse speed is each streamline's length over the pair's measured delay.

Data were provided [in part] by the Human Connectome Project, WU-Minn Consortium (Principal Investigators: David Van Essen and Kamil Ugurbil; 1U54MH091657) funded by the 16 NIH Institutes and Centers that support the NIH Blueprint for Neuroscience Research; and by the McDonnell Center for Systems Neuroscience at Washington University.

The Glasser, tract, thalamic-nuclei and Brainnetome layers are derived from HCP data and are shared under the WU-Minn HCP Open Access Data Use Terms. Each dataset keeps its own license: FreeSurfer, MNI ICBM 152, Brainnetome (non-commercial), HCP, CC BY 4.0 (Lausanne, MASSP 2.0, MIAL, HCP-MMP1 on fsaverage, 80 bundles) and MIT (Schaefer). Every file here is converted and simplified from the original.

Rendering is three.js; hover picking uses three-mesh-bvh. For learning and exploring, not for clinical use.
